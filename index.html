FireNomics

FireNomics runs expected-value math on FIRE decisions, and one of its guides puts a $997 guru course next to 15 years of index returns. The same arithmetic works on any course. Before asking what the material teaches, it pays to price what the tuition could have bought.

Every course fee carries a second number

The 4% rule turns one-time expenses into income machines. Divide $997 by 0.04 and the tuition hides a $24,925 portfolio, because that is what it costs to pay yourself $997 every year instead. Invested once at a 6 percent real return, the same $997 grows to about $2,390 over 15 years.

The habit is where the math gets loud

One course is easy to shrug off. A $997 course every year, put into an index fund at a 6 percent real return, builds about $23,200 over the same 15 years. Under the 4% rule that pays $928 annually, every year. The buying habit refunds nearly its own cost annually, and the principal stays invested.

The bar the sales page has to clear

That is the expected-value question the guide on firenomics.com asks, now with a number on it. The material has to beat $928 a year to justify its sticker, because skipping one course and investing the fee buys almost the same stream on autopilot. Most course sales pages never put a competing number that concrete on the table.
